\begin{abstract}
This report presents a reproducible clustering study of hotel booking demand data
(117,430 records, course release v1) with the goal of partitioning bookings into
interpretable guest segments based solely on pre-stay observable features.
Two representations are studied: a value-inclusive pipeline
(\texttt{R-EUCLID-robust-weighted-withADR}) and a purely behavioural pipeline that
excludes the daily rate (\texttt{R-EUCLID-robust-weighted-noADR}).
K-Means ($k = 3$, Silhouette $= 0.452$, ARI $= 0.989 \pm 0.007$ across 10 seeds),
iK-Means (automatic $k = 8$), and Agglomerative Hierarchical clustering (Ward,
Silhouette $= 0.453$ at $k = 3$) are compared under a unified evaluation protocol.
The selected three-cluster solution identifies a Non-Refundable Advance Booker segment
(12.4\%), a Standard Flexible City Guest segment (70.7\%), and a Premium Resort
Long-Stay segment (16.8\%).
The noADR comparison confirms that the commitment axis (\texttt{is\_non\_refund})
drives cluster separation independently of price.
Key limitations include the absence of ground-truth labels, the use of an ordinal
meal-plan encoding, and the fact that clustering is performed at booking level rather
than guest level.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} hotel booking demand; clustering; guest segmentation; mixed-type
data; reproducibility; K-Means; Ward linkage

\section{Introduction}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection*{Context}
Hotel revenue management depends on understanding who books, when, and how.
Booking records carry rich pre-arrival signals --- rate, stay duration, lead time,
deposit commitment, meal plan, parking need, and special requests --- that may
reveal latent guest types with distinct spending patterns and service preferences.
Identifying such types without relying on post-stay outcomes enables targeted pricing,
upselling, and capacity planning decisions at the time of booking.

\subsection*{Segmentation question}
\textit{Can hotel booking records be partitioned into distinct, interpretable
behavioural segments using only pre-stay observable characteristics --- daily rate,
total estimated spend, length of stay, booking horizon, meal plan, deposit
commitment, special requests, and parking need --- to identify guest profiles that
support targeted revenue management and personalised service strategies?}

\subsection*{Clustering rationale}
No ground-truth segment labels exist; the goal is discovery rather than
classification, making supervised methods inapplicable. Booking records exhibit
substantial heterogeneity in ADR (\texteuro 0--5{,}400), stay duration (0--69
nights), and lead time (0--737 days), suggesting meaningful subgroups. Clustering
inputs are restricted to variables known at booking time (leakage control); post-event
fields are reserved for post-hoc profiling only.

\subsection*{Contribution}
We implement an end-to-end reproducible pipeline covering mixed-type preprocessing,
K-Means and Hierarchical Ward clustering, iK-Means initialisation, internal validity
assessment (Silhouette, Calinski-Harabasz, Davies-Bouldin), seed-stability analysis
(ARI), scaler sensitivity, a PCA representation study (Module E4), and a noADR
representation comparison. All results are logged in \texttt{experiments.csv} and
regenerable via \texttt{run\_all.py}.

\subsection{Data quality and feature governance}

\textbf{Missingness and outliers.}
\texttt{children} has 4 missing values (imputed as 0);
\texttt{country} has 488 missing values (retained for post-hoc profiling only);
\texttt{agent} (16,340) and \texttt{company} (112,593) have high missingness and
are dropped as high-cardinality ID-like fields.
\texttt{adr} has a heavy right tail (max \texteuro 5{,}400) and one negative entry;
\texttt{lead\_time} ranges 0--737 days.

\textbf{Zero-ADR exclusion.}
Rows with \texttt{adr} $= 0$ (1,960 records, corresponding to complementary or
test bookings per dataset documentation) are excluded from clustering, leaving
\textbf{117,430} records. This exclusion is predefined and reported.

\textbf{Leakage control.}
Index time is the booking creation date. The following fields are excluded from
clustering inputs:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{is\_canceled}, \texttt{reservation\_status},
        \texttt{reservation\_status\_date} --- post-decision / post-arrival outcomes
  \item \texttt{agent}, \texttt{company} --- high-cardinality ID fields
  \item \texttt{country} --- retained for post-hoc profiling only
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Feature engineering and encoding.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{total\_nights} $=$ \texttt{stays\_in\_week\_nights} $+$
        \texttt{stays\_in\_weekend\_nights}
  \item \texttt{total\_spend\_log} $= \log(1 + \texttt{adr}) \times
        \texttt{total\_nights}$ --- compressed spend proxy combining rate and
        duration; \texttt{adr\_log} is \emph{not} included separately to avoid
        collinearity
  \item \texttt{is\_non\_refund} $= \mathbf{1}[\texttt{deposit\_type} = \text{``Non
        Refund''}]$ --- binary; replaces a three-level ordinal that inflated
        Euclidean distances
  \item \texttt{lead\_time\_rank} --- \texttt{lead\_time} binned into three
        equal-population quantile groups via \texttt{pd.qcut(q=3)}: 0 =
        last-minute (0--32 d), 1 = mid (33--124 d), 2 = advance ($\geq$125 d).
        The equal-population binning is justified over equal-width binning because
        \texttt{lead\_time} is heavily right-skewed (0--737 days); quantile bins
        ensure each rank level represents a comparable share of bookings, making
        the ordinal distances between levels more meaningful in Euclidean space.
        A log-transformed continuous alternative was considered but the binned
        ordinal representation is preferred here to keep the feature on a bounded
        $[0,2]$ scale consistent with the other ordinal inputs
  \item \texttt{meal\_encoded} --- ordinal encoding SC/Undefined$=0$, BB$=1$,
        HB$=2$, FB$=3$, justified by the monotonically increasing catering
        inclusivity of the four plan levels
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Final representations and distance.}
Two representations are studied:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
ID & Features & Scaler \\
\midrule
\texttt{R-EUCLID-robust-weighted-withADR} &
  \texttt{total\_spend\_log}, \texttt{total\_nights} + 5 binary/ordinal &
  RobustScaler on continuous; raw on binary/ordinal \\
\texttt{R-EUCLID-robust-weighted-noADR} &
  \texttt{total\_nights} + 5 binary/ordinal &
  RobustScaler on continuous; raw on binary/ordinal \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both use Euclidean distance. Feature weights \texttt{meal\_encoded} $\times 0.5$
and \texttt{is\_non\_refund} $\times 3.0$ are applied post-scaling to control
relative contribution; this changes the effective Euclidean metric and is
recorded as preprocessing. All validity indices are computed in the same
weighted space.

\section{Clustering Methods}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\textbf{K-Means (baseline).}
K-Means with \texttt{n\_init=10}, \texttt{max\_iter=300}, \texttt{random\_state=42}.
$k$ range 2--10 predefined based on domain plausibility (2 = minimum meaningful
split; 10 = upper limit beyond which segments lose managerial interpretability).
Final selection: $k = 3$ based on convergence of Silhouette and ARI stability
evidence (see Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\textbf{iK-Means.}
Mirkin's (2005) intelligent initialisation procedure~\cite{mirkin2005} extracts
anomalous clusters greedily from the full 117,430-row matrix with
\texttt{min\_cluster\_size=1000} (predefined: clusters below 1,000 bookings
lack managerial actionability). The retained $k$ is determined automatically.

\textbf{Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering -- Ward linkage.}
Ward linkage minimises within-cluster variance under Euclidean distance, compatible
with the RobustScaler + weighted Euclidean representation.
Run on a reproducible subsample of $n = 30{,}000$ (\texttt{seed=42}), which
represents 25.5\% of the analytic dataset. Subsample impact is quantified via
stability across five subsamples (\texttt{seeds=\{42,7,123,256,999\}}).
$k$ range 2--8; dendrogram cut assessed visually and via validity indices.

\section{Results and Cluster Interpretation}
\label{sec:results}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Data preparation outcomes}

After zero-ADR exclusion: \textbf{117,430 bookings}, 7 features (withADR) or 6
features (noADR). The scaled matrix has shape $(117{,}430 \times 7)$ for withADR
and $(117{,}430 \times 6)$ for noADR. Principal component analysis of the withADR
matrix shows PC1 explains 54.3\% and four components are needed to reach 90\%
variance, confirming moderate linear structure (features are not fully independent).

\textbf{Selected solution: K-Means $k=3$ on withADR.}
Rationale: (i) Silhouette 0.452 exceeds the weak-structure threshold of 0.35
\cite{kaufman1990}; (ii) ARI $0.989 \pm 0.007$ confirms near-identical partitions
across all 10 seeds; (iii) Hierarchical Ward independently finds the same three-
cluster structure (ARI $> 0.92$ against K-Means $k=3$);
(iv) three segments map to actionable business profiles.
iK-Means is not selected as the primary solution because its eight clusters
fragment the three main groups without revealing qualitatively new profiles.

\textbf{Cluster narrative.}
The Non-Refundable Advance Booker is defined by full deposit commitment
(\texttt{is\_non\_refund}$=1.0$ for all members), planned booking horizon (lead-time
rank +63\% above mean), and zero car-parking need --- suggesting a city-hotel guest
who locks in rates early and forfeits flexibility. Despite having the lowest ADR,
this segment represents the highest revenue certainty.

The Standard Flexible City Guest forms the majority segment (70.7\%). It has no
deposit commitment, a wide range of booking horizons, and short stays typical of
business or urban leisure travel. The highest car-parking proportion in this group
(+16\% above mean) suggests a subset of car-driving guests.

The Premium Resort Long-Stay Guest is the smallest but highest-value segment.
Total estimated spend is 136\% above the grand mean, driven by both higher ADR
and substantially longer stays (7.7 nights vs 2.6 dataset average). Resort hotel
is the dominant property type, and above-average special requests (+34\%) indicate
service-oriented guests who may respond well to upsell offers.

\subsection{noADR representation comparison}
\label{sec:noADR}

Removing \texttt{total\_spend\_log} from the feature set and re-clustering tests
whether the withADR clusters are purely price bands. Under the noADR representation:
the Non-Refundable segment (C2) is preserved intact (high \texttt{is\_non\_refund}
still drives separation); the Premium and Standard segments partially merge because
stay duration is the only remaining continuous differentiator without ADR signal.
This confirms that \texttt{is\_non\_refund} provides an independent structural axis,
while \texttt{total\_spend\_log} contributes the price-level separation between
Premium and Standard. The withADR representation is retained as primary because the
research question explicitly includes pricing characteristics.

\section{Discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\textbf{Which result best answers the segmentation question?}
K-Means $k=3$ on the withADR representation best answers the question. It produces
three stable, interpretable segments (ARI $0.989$) that align with intuitive booking
archetypes: commitment-driven planners, standard flexible guests, and high-value
leisure visitors. Hierarchical Ward independently recovers the same structure,
strengthening confidence in the solution.

\textbf{How did preprocessing choices influence results?}
The \texttt{is\_non\_refund} $\times 3.0$ weight is the single most consequential
preprocessing decision: without it, the Non-Refundable segment does not emerge
cleanly because the binary feature's natural scale (0/1) is dominated by the
continuous features. The scaler sensitivity analysis shows that switching from
RobustScaler to StandardScaler on numerical features produces only a marginal
silhouette change, confirming robustness to this choice. MinMax scaling on all
features (including binary) artificially inflates scores and is not appropriate here.

\textbf{Are the clusters coherent, stable, and useful?}
Coherence: Silhouette 0.452 is above the weak-structure threshold; intra-cluster
variance of the Non-Refundable segment is the lowest (0.988), confirming it is the
tightest cluster. The Premium segment has the highest intra-cluster variance (5.483),
reflecting heterogeneity in stay lengths and rates within the high-spend group.
Stability: near-perfect ARI across seeds. Interpretability: each segment maps to a
distinct strategic action (yield management for Non-Refundable; upselling for
Premium; flexibility-based conversion for Standard).

\textbf{Decision-relevant recommendations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Non-Refundable Advance Booker}: offer early-bird rate locks and
        package upgrades at booking time; cancellation risk is minimal.
  \item \textbf{Standard Flexible City Guest}: targeted last-minute promotional
        rates and parking-inclusive packages may increase conversion.
  \item \textbf{Premium Resort Long-Stay}: personalised pre-arrival upsells (room
        upgrades, spa, F\&B packages) given above-average special requests.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What should not be concluded.}
Clusters are defined at booking level, not guest level; a returning guest may
appear in different segments across stays. The absence of ground-truth labels means
segment validity cannot be externally verified. The premium ADR separation between
C0 and C1 may partly reflect hotel type (resort vs city) rather than guest
willingness-to-pay. Finally, the Manual weights (\texttt{is\_non\_refund} $\times 3$,
\texttt{meal} $\times 0.5$) embed subjective assumptions that should be revisited
with domain expert input.

\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Reproducibility}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\textbf{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item No ground-truth segment labels; internal validity indices measure geometric
        compactness, not business relevance.
  \item Unit of analysis is the booking record, not the guest; one guest may appear
        in multiple segments across stays.
  \item Ordinal meal-plan encoding assumes equal spacing between plan levels; a
        one-hot encoding would be more conservative.
  \item \texttt{lead\_time\_rank} uses quantile binning which may lose information
        relative to a log-transformed continuous feature.
  \item The dataset covers two Portuguese hotels (2015--2017); generalisability to
        other markets or time periods is untested.
  \item iK-Means runtime was prohibitive on the full dataset in the project
        timeline; results should be treated as exploratory.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reproducibility.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Repository tag: \texttt{Submission}
  \item Experiment log: \texttt{experiments.csv} (schema: \texttt{date/run\_id},
        \texttt{representation\_id}, \texttt{model}, \texttt{parameters},
        \texttt{seed}, \texttt{silhouette}, \texttt{calinski}, \texttt{davies},
        \texttt{diagnostics}, \texttt{notes})
  \item Environment: \texttt{environment.yml}
  \item Entry point: \texttt{run\_all.py} executes the full notebook end-to-end
        via \texttt{nbconvert}
  \item Raw data: not committed; obtain from course platform, place in
        \texttt{data/hotel\_bookings\_course\_release\_v1.csv}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Ethics and governance.}
Dataset used in accordance with course release terms. No personal identifiers are
present; country of origin is used only for post-hoc profiling. Raw data not
committed to the repository.

\textbf{Academic integrity.}
Claude Code (Anthropic) was consulted during development for methodological
discussion, syntax review, and code structuring. All analytical decisions,
model choices, interpretations, and written content were produced and validated by
the team. The tool was not used to generate substantive analyses or report text
autonomously.

\section{Conclusion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This study partitioned 117,430 hotel booking records into three stable guest
segments using K-Means clustering on a mixed-type pre-stay feature set.
The three-cluster solution (Silhouette $= 0.452$, ARI $= 0.989 \pm 0.007$
across 10 seeds, corroborated by independent Hierarchical Ward clustering)
identifies a Non-Refundable Advance Booker, a Standard Flexible City Guest, and
a Premium Resort Long-Stay Guest --- each with distinct revenue management
implications. The noADR comparison confirms that booking-commitment behaviour
(\texttt{is\_non\_refund}) drives cluster separation independently of price.
Future work should validate segments against actual revenue outcomes, test
alternative meal-plan encodings, and extend the analysis to multi-hotel or
multi-year datasets.
